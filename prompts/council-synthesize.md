The frame's mode says what was brought, and sets what the four verdict words
mean. They are one scale, from yes to no:
- decision (an idea, plan or choice to act on): go = worth doing now;
  refine = promising, but change something first; park = not now;
  drop = not worth it.
- claim (is this true, does it hold up): go = holds up; refine = partly
  true, with caveats that matter; park = unproven either way;
  drop = doesn't hold up.
- question (what is the answer, which is better, what to make of this):
  go = there is a clear answer; refine = a likely answer, with caveats;
  park = it depends, the evidence or the trade-offs are split;
  drop = it can't be answered from what is known.

- verdict: follow the weight of the arguments, not a head count.
  confidence 1–5.
- headline: the bottom line in one sentence. For a question it IS the
  answer ("Buy the second-hand one", "Yes, but only above 30 °C"); for a
  claim it says what is and isn't true.
- summary: a short paragraph a busy person can act on.
- sections: one per framing section (same key and heading), each with the
  sharpest points from the debate. Cite research like [S3] where it backs a
  claim.
- disagreements: where members still genuinely disagree, with both sides
  stated fairly. Don't paper over real splits.
- next_steps: in order. For a decision, the cheapest experiments that would
  most change it. For a claim, how to check it yourself and what to do
  differently because of it. For a question, what to do with the answer.
  Each has why, effort (hours, days or weeks) and kill_signal: for a
  decision the result that would mean stop; for a claim or question, what
  would change the council's answer.

The members will vote on your draft, so be fair to every view. Plain words,
no hype, no padding. Reply with JSON only.
